## 1. Purpose of this document

This document captures the strategic rationale behind the Nigeria National Single Window (NSW) Time Release Study (TRS) Intelligence & Trade Assurance Platform — the "why" behind the dashboard. It is the source narrative for the **About** tab in the TRS prototype and the basis for the programme's TODO list.

## 2. The problem the platform diagnoses

Periodic WCO-style TRS exercises are **necessary but insufficient**.

Nigeria's trade clearance performance cannot be understood — or improved — through annual or bi-annual snapshot studies alone. The gap between claimed reform and measured reality persists because:

- **Measurement is episodic**, not continuous. Between formal TRS cycles, there is no systematic way to know whether dwell times are improving, stagnating, or regressing.
- **Attribution is absent.** When a consignment takes five days, nobody can say with confidence whether the delay sits with an agency permit, payment validation, terminal congestion, or trader documentation.
- **Assurance is missing.** Claims of "24-hour clearance" or "18% improvement" lack independent verification against shipment-level evidence.
- **Data quality is uneven.** Incomplete timestamps on early journey stages (vessel arrival → examination start) create blind spots precisely where delays are often largest.

## 3. What the platform provides

A continuous, evidence-based performance layer that:

1. **Measures** end-to-end release time from vessel/aircraft arrival to gate-out.
2. **Attributes** delays to specific agencies, processes, or causes.
3. **Generates** agency scorecards, port comparisons, and facilitation benchmarks (including peer ports Lomé, Tema, Dakar).
4. **Uses AI** for anomaly detection and pattern recognition across cargo types and time windows.
5. **Supports** corrective-action workflows with named owners, deadlines, and measurable outcomes.
6. **Provides** independent assurance that claimed improvements are real — statistically, not rhetorically.

## 4. Alignment with international frameworks

| Framework | Relevance |
|-----------|-----------|
| **WCO TRS Guide v4 (2025)** | Methodology backbone: event model, median as primary metric, 95th percentile for tail risk, timestamp completeness as data-quality indicator, stage medians need not sum to overall median |
| **WTO TFA Article 7.6** | Mandates TRS as a transparency and facilitation commitment |
| **AfCFTA** | Trade competitiveness and cost-of-doing-business objectives |
| **NNSW / NRS digital architecture** | The platform leverages existing NSW and Customs systems as primary data feeds rather than building parallel silos |

## 5. Strategic North Star

**24-hour cargo clearance** for the majority of low-risk consignments, measured continuously and attributed to specific interventions.

Near-term trajectory (6–12 months): 25–40% reduction in average release time through joint inspection scheduling, expanded non-intrusive inspection, and NNSW-driven process synchronisation.

Medium-term: approach 48-hour One-Stop-Shop / AEO levels for a growing share of compliant traffic.

## 6. Economic stakes

- Lower demurrage and inventory carrying costs for traders
- Reduced diversion of Nigerian-bound cargo to neighbouring ports (Lomé, Tema)
- Improved AfCFTA competitiveness and FDI attractiveness in logistics and manufacturing
- Higher revenue yield through faster, more transparent processing

## 7. Governance implications

- **Institutional home:** NNSW Secretariat with NRS / Customs, formal participation of OGAs and terminal operators
- **Assurance independence:** periodic external validation to maintain credibility of performance claims
- **Change management:** agency scorecards and public dashboards create constructive pressure; AEO expansion and risk-based selectivity (target <10% physical exam) are high-ROI levers
- **Key risk:** the classic "measurement without consequence" trap — mitigation requires clear ownership of corrective actions linked to PEBEC / Ease of Doing Business scorecards
